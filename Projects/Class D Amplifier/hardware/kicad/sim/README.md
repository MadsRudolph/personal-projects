# Class D simulation testbenches

One KiCad sheet per block of the amplifier. Open a `.kicad_pro` in KiCad, then
**Inspect -> Simulator** and press Run: each sheet carries its own analysis
command, both as text on the sheet and in its `.wbk` workbook.

| sheet | what it answers |
|---|---|
| `sim_a_vground` | does the 6 V virtual ground come up and settle? |
| `sim_b_triangle` | is the carrier the right frequency and amplitude? |
| `sim_c_input` | are AUDIO_P and AUDIO_N truly equal and opposite? |
| `sim_d_pwm` | does duty track the audio, and stay complementary? |
| `sim_e_driver` | is the dead time right, and does the bootstrap charge? |
| `sim_f_bridge` | how much power reaches 4 Ω, and how much carrier? |
| `sim_g_chain` | audio in to speaker out — does the design meet the brief? |

Everything is generated by `tools/sim_layout.py`; edit that rather than the
sheets. The driver, bridge and filter are drawn once and translated onto E, F
and G, with the geometry lifted from the matching block of the board's own
`tools/classd_layout.py` — a bootstrap wired one way on the board and another
way in its testbench proves nothing.

## Models

`models/classd_sim.lib` holds behavioural subcircuits for the four parts no one
ships a model for — `OPAMP_TL074`, `CMP_LM311`, `INV_CD4049`, `DRV_HIP4082` —
each parameterised with the datasheet figures recorded in `docs/BUILD-NOTES.md`.
They use only primitives every ngspice understands (B/E/G sources, switches and
lossless T-lines for delay), deliberately avoiding the XSPICE digital models
KiCad's own class-D demo relies on, because those are not compiled into every
ngspice build.

The IRF540N is the exception: `models/IRF-Power-VDMOS.mod` is the real VDMOS
card KiCad ships, body diode included.

## Verifying

`tools/test_models.py` checks each model against its datasheet numbers.
`tools/run_sims.py` exports each sheet through `kicad-cli` and asserts the
numbers the design is supposed to produce. Both need the miniconda interpreter
that has PySpice:

```
C:\Users\Mads2\miniconda3\python.exe tools\test_models.py
C:\Users\Mads2\miniconda3\python.exe tools\run_sims.py
```

These are a harness for catching broken testbenches, not part of the
deliverable — the sheets themselves run in KiCad's own simulator.

## What the simulations found

- **At full output the amplifier is exactly on the TL074's common-mode limit.**
  Bench G makes 13 W across 4 Ω, and at that level AUDIO_P bottoms out at
  4.00 V — which is the TL074's specified V−+4 V input floor, to two decimal
  places. The 12 W target and the input stage's limit arrive together, with no
  headroom in hand. This is the risk `docs/BUILD-NOTES.md` flagged on paper,
  and the simulation puts a number on it: turn the level pot past this point
  and the distortion you hear is the buffer, not the bridge.
- **The carrier lands at ~215 kHz, not the 250 kHz the arithmetic predicts.**
  The LM311's 165 ns propagation delay adds to every half period, roughly
  330 ns per cycle. `RV1` covers it easily, which is exactly why the brief
  asked for a trimmer — but do not expect the calculated value to be the one
  you set.
- **The dead time comes out at 225 ns for R12 = 3k3**, against the 200 ns read
  off the datasheet curve, and the two driver outputs are never high together.
  The two CD4049 stages differ by one gate delay, so a leg's two *inputs* are
  high together for about 25 ns on every edge — harmless, because the HIP4082
  turns both outputs off for that state, and far below the dead time.
- **The bootstrap caps take about 50 µs to fill** from cold, and settle
  0.22 V below the rail — one Schottky drop. Benches E, F and G all discard
  the first part of the run for that reason.
- **Residual carrier on the output is 0.28 Vpp at a fixed duty and 0.36 Vpp
  with audio**, against the brief's ~0.4 Vpp worst case and BUILD-NOTES'
  0.2–0.25 Vpp nominal. The real figure sits between the two.
- The virtual ground takes about 2.5 s to settle through R1||R2 into C1. Worth
  knowing before deciding a board is dead at power-on.

## Known scorer result

`sch_score.py` passes on six of the seven sheets. `sim_d_pwm` fails on ratios
rather than on anything drawn wrong: it is an eight-part sheet whose whole job
is to feed three named nets into two comparators, so the label fraction and the
four deliberately no-connected `BAL`/`STRB` pins are a large share of a small
total. Its ERC is clean and its five behaviour checks pass.
